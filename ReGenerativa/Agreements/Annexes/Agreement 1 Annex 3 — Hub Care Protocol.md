*Annex 3 to the [[Agreement 1 — Ownership|Ownership Agreement]]. Sets out the full division of responsibility for caring for the Asset — both ongoing maintenance during use and end-of-stay cleaning standards. Forms an integral part of that Agreement. Also referenced in the [[Agreement 3 — Stewardship|Stewardship Agreement]] (Clause 10).*

*This Protocol consolidates what were previously two separate annexes (Maintenance and Cleaning & Restoration). The split was procedural; the practice is one continuous act of care.*

---

## Section A — Purpose and Principle

This Protocol describes how the Asset is cared for: kept in good working condition through ongoing attention, restored to clean and orderly state after each use, and protected against decay through structural maintenance when needed.

The work of caring for a place is not a list of tasks. It is a relationship — between the Owner who has entrusted the Asset, the Association which uses it, the Steward who coordinates that use, and the participants who pass through. This Protocol gives that relationship a shared shape so that everyone knows what they are responsible for and what they can expect from others.

**Who is responsible for what:**

- **Ongoing maintenance** during periods of use → the **Association** (typically delegated to the Steward)
- **Extraordinary maintenance** (structural, infrastructural) → the **Owner**
- **End-of-stay cleaning and restoration** → the **Steward** for the use period, who may delegate but retains overall responsibility
- **Tools and equipment** (registration, maintenance, lifecycle) → the **Steward** as register-keeper; user responsibility scales with ownership status; donations registered with the donor's A5-1 Support Schedule
- **Damage reporting** → whoever discovers it, or creates it, immediately

---

## Section B — Definitions

**Ordinary maintenance:** Routine upkeep needed to preserve the Asset in good working condition through normal use. Responsibility of the Association during periods of use (pro rata where relevant).

**Extraordinary maintenance:** Substantial repairs or replacements due to structural issues, wear beyond ordinary use, ageing infrastructure, or unforeseen damage. Responsibility of the Owner, to be carried out within a reasonable time after notification by the Association.

**End-of-stay cleaning:** The set of tasks that restore the Asset to clean, orderly, and safe condition at the conclusion of any stay, event, or use period. Responsibility of the Steward for that use period.

**Hub Asset:** Tool or equipment owned by the Association for use at this Hub.

**Personal Tool at Hub:** Tool owned by a specific Steward, Member, or Mentor and kept at this Hub during their period of presence.

**Donated Tool:** Tool transferred to the Hub via the Sponsorship Agreement (Agreement 5); ownership rests with the Association from the date of registration.

## Section C — Ongoing Maintenance (Association's Responsibility)

The following tasks are the responsibility of the Association during its periods of use, typically carried out by the Steward and contributing participants. Tools and equipment are governed separately under Section F.

**Cleaning during stays:**
- Daily common area tidying
- Kitchen cleaning after each use (surfaces, appliances, floor)
- Bathroom cleaning after each use (toilets, showers, sinks, floors)
- *(End-of-stay deep clean is governed separately in Section G)*

**Grounds and outdoor spaces:**
- Grass cutting and pathway maintenance: [frequency — e.g. weekly during growing season]
- Weeding of kitchen garden and paths: [e.g. as needed]
- Removal of waste and compost management
- Pruning of hedges and small trees (not structural pruning): [e.g. once annually]

**Minor indoor repairs:**
- Replacing lightbulbs and fuses
- Minor plumbing (unblocking sinks, replacing tap washers)
- Touching up paintwork after minor scuffs or marks
- Re-hanging doors or cupboard fronts that have come loose
- Sealing minor tile cracks (cosmetic only — not structural)
- Replacing broken window catches (not frames)

**Utilities management:**
- Monitoring energy and water consumption
- Reporting unusual consumption patterns to the Association coordinator
- Ensuring all utilities are switched off / secured at end of each use period

**Reporting obligation:** The Association shall notify the Owner promptly (and in any event within [e.g. 5 business days]) of any defect, damage, or maintenance need that goes beyond ordinary maintenance.

---

## Section D — Extraordinary Maintenance (Owner's Responsibility)

The following are examples of extraordinary maintenance that remain the Owner's responsibility and cost:

- Roof repair or replacement
- Structural wall repair or consolidation
- Foundation work
- Full replacement of boiler, heating system, or water heater
- Full replacement of electrical wiring or distribution board
- Full window or door frame replacement
- Septic tank or drainage system overhaul
- Major tree surgery (felling, heavy pruning of large trees)
- Replacement of flooring (not cosmetic repair)

**Response time after notification:** The Owner commits to commencing extraordinary maintenance within [e.g. 30] days of receiving written notification from the Association, except in cases of genuine unavailability (in which case an agreed timeline shall be communicated promptly).

---

## Section E — Shared and Ambiguous Cases

Where a maintenance task falls on the boundary between ordinary and extraordinary, the Parties shall determine responsibility in good faith based on: the cause of the need (normal use vs. structural failure), the cost involved, and the urgency. If no agreement is reached within [e.g. 15] days, the dispute resolution procedure under GT-7 of the General Terms applies.

## Section F — Tools & Equipment Register and Care

This section governs all tools, equipment, vehicles, and appliances kept at the Hub. Unlike facility care, tools have individual lives — they arrive, get used, get serviced, sometimes break, sometimes leave. The register makes those lives legible. The categories that follow distinguish what changes when ownership status changes.

### F.1 Register

The Steward maintains a single Tool & Equipment Register for the Hub. Each entry records:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Item ID | T-001, T-002, etc. — issued at registration |
| Description | Make, model, distinguishing features |
| Category | Hand tool / Power tool / Vehicle / Appliance / Equipment |
| Ownership status | Hub Asset / Personal Tool at Hub / Donated Tool / Borrowed |
| Acquired date and source | When and how it arrived; if donated, the donor's A5-1 reference |
| Location at hub | Where it is kept |
| Condition | New / Good / Wear / Needs maintenance / Out of service |
| Maintenance schedule | If applicable: e.g. annual sharpening, monthly lubrication, biannual calibration |
| Last serviced | Date and by whom |
| Next service due | Date or trigger condition |
| Notes | Anything else relevant — borrowing restrictions, safety guidance, accessory list |

The register is reviewed at each periodic Stewardship review meeting (Stewardship Agreement, Clause 14.2).

### F.2 Ownership Patterns

Maintenance liability and on-hub status vary by ownership:

- **Hub Asset** — owned by the Association. Steward maintains; replacement budgeted through Hub operating costs or via Sponsorship.
- **Personal Tool at Hub** — owned by a specific Steward, Member, or Mentor and kept on-site during their period of presence. Maintenance responsibility remains with the owner. Removed when the owner departs, or transferred to Hub Asset status by mutual agreement and re-registration.
- **Donated Tool** — transferred to the Hub via Agreement 5. Ownership rests with the Association from the date of registration. Donor's A5-1 entry referenced; recognition follows A5-2 if applicable.
- **Borrowed Tool** — temporarily on-site under a written or recorded informal arrangement. Owner remains liable for maintenance; Hub liable for safe storage and timely return.

Damage to a Personal Tool or Borrowed Tool while in legitimate Hub use is treated as a shared matter resolved between the user, the Steward, and the owner — escalated under Section H Damage Reporting if not resolved amicably.

### F.3 Maintenance Practices

Tools are not all maintained on the same rhythm. The following categorisation applies:

- **Routine post-use cleaning** — applies to all tools after each use. User responsibility.
- **Daily-use tools** (kitchen knives, hand tools in regular rotation) — sharpened or serviced as needed by the user, with weekly check by Steward.
- **Periodic-use tools** (scythes, pruning equipment, occasional power tools) — sharpened and serviced seasonally; logged in the register.
- **Mechanical equipment** (pumps, motors, generators, mowers) — lubricated, fuelled, and inspected per manufacturer guidance or local practice; logged in the register.
- **Power tools** — calibration and safety check at the interval specified per item; PPE checked alongside the tool.
- **Vehicles** — service interval per manufacturer; insurance and roadworthiness checks tracked in the register notes.

### F.4 Lifecycle Events

- **New tool arriving at Hub** — registered within **14 days** of arrival. Item ID assigned. Ownership status declared at registration.
- **Tool donated under Agreement 5** — registered as Donated Tool; A5-1 reference recorded.
- **Tool retirement** — flagged as Out of service in the register; held for **30 days** to allow replacement decision before disposal or transfer to another Hub.
- **Replacement** — funded through Hub operating costs (Steward to flag in budget); or via fresh sponsorship under A5; or via inter-hub transfer if another Hub has a surplus.
- **Inter-hub borrowing** — permitted at Steward's discretion subject to the Hub DNA conditions of both Hubs. Logged in the register (note: borrowed-out date and borrowing Hub).
- **Loss or theft** — reported under Section H Damage Reporting and to the Association.

### F.5 Cross-References

- Donated tools: cross-reference the donor's [[Agreement 5 Annex 1 — Support Schedule|A5-1 Support Schedule]] entry
- Damage to tools: handled under Section H Damage Reporting of this Annex
- Servicing events: recorded in Section J Maintenance Log under the Tool Servicing Events sub-table
- The Tool & Equipment Register itself is filed alongside the Hub's other operational records, not separately

## Section G — End-of-Stay Cleaning Protocol

This protocol ensures that the Asset is left in a clean, orderly, and safe condition at the end of each stay, event, or use period — consistent with the Regenerative Covenant (GT-2) and duty of care under Clause 3.1 of the Ownership Agreement.

**Responsibility:** The designated Steward for the use period, who may delegate tasks to participants but retains overall responsibility.

**Process:** The Steward completes and signs off the checklist below before departure. A copy is retained in the maintenance log (Section J).

### Kitchen

☐ All surfaces wiped down (countertops, hob, oven exterior)
☐ Oven and hob cleaned inside (if used for cooking residue)
☐ Fridge cleared of perishable items and wiped inside
☐ Dishes washed and stored (or dishwasher run and emptied)
☐ Sink cleaned and unblocked
☐ Food waste composted or disposed of correctly
☐ Bins emptied and bags replaced
☐ Floor swept and mopped
☐ Gas / electrical appliances switched off

### Bathrooms

☐ Toilets cleaned (bowl, seat, exterior)
☐ Showers and baths cleaned (tiles, tray, fixtures)
☐ Sinks and taps cleaned
☐ Mirrors wiped
☐ Floors mopped
☐ Towels collected for washing (or left as agreed with Owner)
☐ Supplies checked and noted if running low (soap, toilet paper, cleaning products)

### Bedrooms and Sleeping Areas

☐ Beds stripped (sheets and pillowcases collected for washing or left as agreed)
☐ Surfaces dusted
☐ Windows closed and latched
☐ Floors swept or vacuumed
☐ All personal items removed by participants

### Common Areas (Living Room, Dining, Workshop, etc.)

☐ Furniture returned to original or agreed configuration
☐ All equipment and tools stored in designated locations
☐ Floors swept / vacuumed / mopped
☐ Doors and windows closed and locked
☐ Any borrowed or rearranged items returned

### Outdoor Areas

☐ Outdoor furniture returned to stored or agreed position
☐ BBQ / fire pit area cleaned (ash disposed of safely when cold)
☐ Waste sorted and placed in correct bins / composting system
☐ Paths and entry area clear of debris
☐ Gates closed and locked (where applicable)

### Utilities and Security

☐ All lights switched off
☐ Heating / cooling set to agreed standby level (or switched off)
☐ Water supply isolation valve checked (if applicable in winter)
☐ All windows and external doors locked
☐ Keys returned to designated location or person
☐ Alarm (if applicable) set per agreed protocol

## Section H — Damage Reporting

Any damage, breakage, or defect discovered during the clean-up or at any other time must be reported immediately to the Association coordinator, with:

- Description of the damage
- Photograph (if possible)
- Assessment of cause (accidental, wear, unknown)

The Association will notify the Owner per Clause 3.5 of the Ownership Agreement. For damage to tools or equipment specifically, see also Section F.2 (ownership-dependent resolution patterns).

## Section I — Ecological Restoration Standards

In keeping with the Association's regenerative mandate, the following standards apply beyond basic cleanliness:

- **Waste sorting:** All waste to be sorted into correct streams (organic / recyclable / residual) per local municipality rules. Location of bins: [specify]
- **Composting:** Food waste and organic matter to be added to [composting system location]. No meat, cooked food, or dairy in outdoor composters unless system is appropriate.
- **Water:** Report any leaks, dripping taps, or unusual consumption before departure.
- **Energy:** All unnecessary electrical loads disconnected. Solar systems left in [agreed standby mode].
- **Consumables:** Record any supplies depleted (cleaning products, toilet paper, firewood, etc.) in the maintenance log so they can be replenished before the next use period.
