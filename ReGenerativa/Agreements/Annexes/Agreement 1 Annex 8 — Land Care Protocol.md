*Annex 8 to the [[Agreement 1 — Ownership|Ownership Agreement]]. The **land care protocol** — the asset-specific child of the general [[Agreement 1 Annex 6 — Asset Care Protocol]] for the case where the Asset is land cared for as land (a field, orchard, woodland, garden, or parcel) rather than as a gathering place. Forms an integral part of that Agreement; also referenced in the [[Agreement 3 — Stewardship|Stewardship Agreement]] (Clause 10). Final framings await F1.* *(Pending F1.)*

> **Read alongside [[Agreement 1 Annex 6 — Asset Care Protocol]].** The general parent holds what is true of caring for any Asset — the responsibility split (ordinary vs extraordinary; Steward / Association / Owner / user; the shared burden of [[Agreement 2 — Membership]] §4.6), the Asset register and condition, the maintenance cadence and log, damage reporting, and lifecycle. This child adds what *land* specifically needs: working *with* living systems over seasonal and multi-year cycles, where "care" means leaving the soil, water, and biodiversity more alive than they were found (the Regenerative Covenant, GT-2). Where a parcel is **both** worked land **and** a gathering place, this protocol applies to its land and the [[Agreement 1 Annex 3 — Hub Care Protocol]] to its place. Care is purpose-bound to the Asset's **Asset DNA** ([[Agreement 1 Annex 2 — Permitted Use and Regenerative Impact]]).

---

## Section A — Scope and Principle

This Protocol applies to any Asset registered (A1-6 §C) as **Land** and cared for as land. Unlike a building or a tool, land is not "maintained" to a fixed state — it is *tended* toward greater vitality. The Steward works with its cycles, not against them: the measure of good care is not tidiness but the trend of the living systems (soil, water, biodiversity) over time.

"Good condition" for land is defined by its **Asset DNA** (A1-2) — the permitted uses and limitations the Owner set, and the regenerative-impact dimensions the parcel is meant to serve. A vineyard excluded from Association use, a woodland to be left wild, a field for market gardening: each carries different care, set by its DNA.

---

## Section B — What Land Care Covers

Tended on natural cycles, recorded against the register and log of the parent (A1-6 §C–D):

- **Soil** — building organic matter, protecting structure, minimising bare soil and compaction; composting and mulching; no practice that degrades the soil for short-term yield.
- **Water** — protecting watercourses and water quality; rainwater capture and slowing runoff; irrigation only within sustainable limits; reporting leaks, erosion, or pollution.
- **Biodiversity** — protecting and extending habitat (hedgerows, margins, pollinator forage, deadwood, water features); favouring native and diverse plantings; recording species seen.
- **Vegetation and crops** — planting, pruning, harvesting, and rotation appropriate to the parcel and season; **structural** tree work (felling, heavy pruning of large trees) is extraordinary and the Owner's responsibility (A1-6 §B).
- **Inputs** — preferring regenerative methods; chemical inputs only where permitted by the Asset DNA and applicable law, recorded.
- **Boundaries and access** — keeping gates, fences, and paths sound so the parcel is safe and secure; respecting any access reserved to the Owner (A1 §2).

## Section C — Seasonal Cadence

Land care follows the season rather than a fixed service interval. The Steward keeps a **seasonal works plan** — what is attended to in each season — aligned where useful with the network's equinox/solstice coordination points (A3 §14.2):

| Season | Typical works (adapt to the parcel and its DNA) |
|---|---|
| Spring | Sowing/planting, soil preparation, early growth care, habitat checks before nesting |
| Summer | Watering within limits, weeding, harvesting, fire/drought watch, pollinator support |
| Autumn | Harvest, seed saving, mulching, planting trees/perennials, composting |
| Winter | Structural/dormant pruning, infrastructure repair, planning, leaving habitat undisturbed |

The plan and what was actually done are recorded in the maintenance log (A1-6 §D).

## Section E — Responsibility, Damage, and Lifecycle

- **Ordinary tending** (the works above) → the **Association** via the **Steward**, met from the Asset holon's cost-coverage bucket (GT-4.4(b)).
- **Extraordinary** works (major drainage, large-tree surgery, restoration after a significant event) → the **Owner**, per A1-6 §B.
- **Damage / loss / pollution / significant pest or disease** → reported immediately (A1-6 §E), and to the Owner per A1 §3.5.
- **Lifecycle / handover** — at tenure handover the Steward records the parcel's current condition, the seasonal works in progress, and what the next Steward needs to know, so living continuity is not lost (A1-6 §F; A3 handover).
