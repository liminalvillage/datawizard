*Governance protocol for the hardship support function of the Network Regeneration Fund. Addresses the gap in the current constellation: the Fund explicitly allocates to "steward/contributor hardship support" (GT-4.2) but no document governs how this works in practice. This protocol does not require a new agreement — it operates under the Association's governance powers and the General Terms.*

*This document is internal governance, not a contract. It is approved by the Association's Board and reviewed annually.*

---

## Preamble

A network that redistributes economic value only upward — to infrastructure, digital systems, and external partners — while leaving its stewards and contributors exposed to personal hardship has not understood the holonic principle. Every part sustains the whole, and the whole must sustain its parts.

The Care Protocol governs how the Network Regeneration Fund's hardship support allocation reaches the people who need it. It is designed to be simple, humane, and resistant to the two failure modes that plague most such systems: the bureaucratic (forms, committees, delays that discourage use) and the relational (informal favouritism that erodes trust).

The protocol rests on three principles:

**Proximity** — decisions are made as close as possible to the person in need, not at the centre.

**Dignity** — the process of asking for support should not itself be a burden. We do not require proof of suffering; we require honesty.

**Reciprocity** — support flows from the commons and is eventually returned to it — not in money, but in continued contribution, in care extended to others, in the gradual strengthening of the whole.

---

## 1. Scope

**Who can apply:**
Any current Member of the Association in good standing who holds or has held an active agreement (Stewardship, Mentorship, Partnership, or Intrapreneurship) within the past 12 months, and is experiencing a material hardship that affects their capacity to continue contributing to the network.

**What can be supported:**
- Emergency living costs (food, utilities, rent arrears) threatening basic stability
- Medical costs not covered by national health provision
- Travel costs that would otherwise prevent participation in a critical hub or network moment
- Childcare or dependent care costs that create a barrier to participation
- Equipment or tool replacement necessary for continued contribution
- Other situations — the list is indicative, not exhaustive. The steward of need defines the need.

**What is not in scope:**
- Business debts or commercial liabilities of ventures or partnerships
- Costs arising from breach of Association agreements
- Recurring income replacement (the Fund is for acute situations, not chronic poverty — chronic poverty requires structural solutions, which the Association should address separately)

## 2. The Process

### Step 1 — Conversation (not an application)

The person experiencing hardship speaks with either:
- Their hub steward (if they have one), or
- A member of the Association's Board they trust

This is a conversation, not an application. No forms. No documentation required at this stage. The conversation has two purposes: to understand the situation clearly, and to identify what support would actually help.

The steward or Board member who receives the conversation holds it in confidence. They do not decide alone — they bring it to the Care Circle (see Clause 3) with only as much detail as necessary, and with the applicant's consent.

### Step 2 — Care Circle Review

The Care Circle reviews the situation and makes a recommendation within **7 days** of the initial conversation. For emergencies (food, medical, housing), the timeline is **48 hours**.

The Care Circle does not investigate or verify. It listens, discusses, and decides in good faith. Deliberate misrepresentation is a matter for the Association's disciplinary process — not something the Care Circle prevents by adding bureaucratic friction.

The recommendation specifies:
- The form of support (cash transfer, goods, services, direct payment to a third party)
- The amount or scope
- Whether any reciprocal commitment is appropriate (see Clause 4)
- Whether any follow-up is needed

### Step 3 — Disbursement

The Association's Board formally approves disbursements over €500 within 5 days of the Care Circle recommendation. Disbursements under €500 are approved by any two Board members within 48 hours.

Payment is made directly to the person, or directly to the relevant party (landlord, medical provider, supplier), according to what was agreed.

## 3. The Care Circle

The Care Circle is a small standing group responsible for receiving and deliberating hardship requests. It is not a committee — it has no bureaucratic status, produces no minutes, and makes no binding decisions. It recommends; the Board approves.

**Composition:**
- 2–3 network members with demonstrated trust and discretion, rotating annually
- At least one Board member (non-voting unless needed for quorum)
- At least one person who has themselves received support from the Fund (once the network is old enough to have this)

**How members are selected:**
Nominated by the Board in consultation with the membership at the annual gathering. The criteria are: trusted by multiple network members, not currently in a position of financial dependency on the Association, able to hold confidence, and willing to serve for one year.

**Confidentiality:**
Care Circle deliberations are strictly confidential. The person's situation is shared only in terms necessary for deliberation. Names are not recorded in any accessible document. The annual report to the membership includes only aggregate numbers — number of disbursements, total amount, broad categories — not individual cases.

---

## 4. Reciprocity

Support from the Fund is not charity and not a loan. It is the commons caring for one of its own. No repayment is required, ever.

What is expected — not as a condition, but as an expression of the same spirit that created the Fund — is continued contribution to the network as capacity allows. The Care Circle may, in its recommendation, suggest a light reciprocal commitment: a skill share, a facilitation session, a piece of documentation. This is always offered, never imposed.

Over time, those who have received support are the network's most important carriers of the Care Protocol's spirit. Their lived experience of being held is what makes others willing to ask, and willing to give.

---

## 5. Fund Management

**Allocation:**
The Network Regeneration Fund (GT-4) receives a minimum of 5% of Net Revenue from all economic activities in the network. Of this, the Board allocates a proportion annually to the Care function. The recommended minimum is **20% of the Fund** (i.e. at least 1% of total network Net Revenue).

**Reserve:**
A minimum care reserve of €[amount — set by Board based on network size] is maintained at all times. If the reserve falls below this level, the Board alerts the membership and may invite voluntary contributions.

**Reporting:**
At each annual gathering, the Board presents:
- Total amount disbursed through the Care Protocol in the year
- Number of disbursements (no names, no cases)
- Remaining reserve
- Any structural patterns emerging (e.g. recurring hardship type suggesting a systemic network issue to address)

---

## 6. Systemic Learning

The Care Protocol is not only a support mechanism — it is a sensor. Patterns of hardship reveal structural weaknesses in the network's economy. If stewards are repeatedly applying because their hubs cannot cover operating costs, that is not a personal failure — it is a signal that the stewardship model needs adjustment. If mentors are applying because programme revenue is too uncertain, that is a signal about programme design.

The Board reviews care patterns annually alongside the financial report. Structural patterns trigger a design review, not just more disbursements.

---

## 7. Review and Evolution

This protocol is reviewed annually at the Association's winter gathering. Any network member may propose amendments. Amendments require Board approval and are recorded in the Decision Log.

The protocol should become simpler over time, not more complex. As trust in the system grows, friction should reduce. As the network matures, the structural conditions that produce hardship should diminish.
