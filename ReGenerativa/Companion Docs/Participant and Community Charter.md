> The charter for the participants and guests the network serves and the communities around its hubs. Its **binding duty** lives in the General Terms (**GT-2.8**); the **complaint route** is the non-member limb of **GT-7.5**; the **mechanics** (feedback channel, complaint handling) live in **Operational Procedures Section O**.

---

## Why this exists

The constellation governs **contributors** — members, stewards, mentors, partners, ventures, owners, elders. But a regenerative hub exists to *serve*: the guest on a retreat, the participant in a programme, the client of a project, and the wider community and land around the place. Until now these people appeared only as project codes in the Ledger and as the object of the access rule — with no stated rights, no voice, and no way to complain. This charter gives them standing. **One can be a participant or guest without being a contributor**; this is the network's promise to those people, distinct from membership.

---

## Part I — Participants and guests

### Who this is for
Anyone the network receives without their needing to hold a role: programme participants, retreat and stay guests, clients of a Project, visitors, and people reached by a hub's activities.

### What we promise

- **Safety and dignity.** Everyone is safeguarded from harm and treated with the dignity floor the network holds for all (GT-2.6; the Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct). Dignity does not depend on contribution.
- **Honest, fair dealing.** Truthful description of any programme or stay, clear pricing and terms before you commit, and fair cancellation and withdrawal rights as required by consumer law (Italian *Codice del Consumo*). No mis-selling; the price is what was stated.
- **Privacy.** Your personal data is handled under GDPR and GT-5; clients appear in the Ledger only as project codes unless you consent to be named (GT-3.4A).
- **Fair reception.** You are received under the network's sliding-scale access rule in good faith (GT-3A.3) — priority and price follow contribution, never unfair discrimination. *(The unconditional no-turn-away floor of GT-3A.8 is a benefit of membership; hubs nonetheless extend hospitality to participants and guests as capacity allows.)*
- **A voice and a way to be heard.** A feedback channel at each hub (Operational Procedures O.1), and a real complaint route: a concern or complaint may be raised through **GT-7.5** (beginning with the host Steward and the Association); a **safety concern** is handled through safeguarding (GT-2.6, Section M). Your statutory rights as a consumer or data subject, and your access to the courts, are never limited by this charter.

### What we ask in return
While you are here, honour the place: respect its Hub DNA covenant, the other people present, the relational standards (no harassment, discrimination, or non-consensual recording), and the care of the space. Being received here is not consumption without care — it is entering a relationship, lightly held.

## Part II — The wider community

Every Hub is a **community ark** (GT-2.3): a node of resilience that operates *alongside* existing local institutions, not against them. Toward the community, watershed, and bioregion in which it sits, the network commits to:

- **No net harm to the place.** A hub does not extract from its neighbours, its watershed, or its bioregion — consistent with the syntropic principle that no value is taken from any part (0.0 §1).
- **Openness and mutual aid.** The hub builds parallel capacity for care and mutual aid with its locality, and is a good neighbour in practice, not only in words.
- **A community voice.** Neighbours and local people may raise a concern through the same route as participants and guests (GT-7.5), and the community's unmet needs can surface as **Network Demand Signals** (GT-1.12) — the basis on which the network responds through intrapreneurship (Agreement 7).
- **Accountability for its place.** A hub's effect on its community, watershed, and bioregion is part of the regenerative assessment (the Regenerative Impact Framework, dimensions B — Human Health and Well-Being, and E — Community and Relational Culture), which feeds the regenerative balance of GT-2.7.
