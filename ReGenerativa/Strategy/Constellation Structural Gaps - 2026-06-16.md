# Constellation Structural Gaps

*A whole-system gap scan run 2026-06-16, asking "what is missing from this constellation?" after the inter-class and developmental passes were applied. It is deliberately complementary to the two prior gap analyses and does not repeat them: the [[Constellation Balance Review - 2026-06-10]] catalogued where the architecture leaves a **class** exposed (X1-X7 + per-agreement gaps, nearly all since applied), and [[Developmental Altitude of the Constellation - 2026-06-06]] catalogued the **developmental** substrate gaps (dignity floor, attunement fallback, empty chair, Elder Emeritus, mostly since applied). Those passes closed the intra-constellation balance. This note names what neither touched: **whole subsystems at the edges that have never been drafted at all.** Companion: [[Constellation FAQ - Concerns by Agreement - 2026-06-10]].*

---

## Framing

The constellation is mature on **who contributes and how value flows** - eight roles, the Unified Fund (GT-4), the Contribution Ledger, dispute resolution, and after the two prior passes, the balance between classes and the relational/turquoise substrate. What is missing is not more of that. It is four edge subsystems the architecture has never named:

1. **The people the hubs serve** have no standing.
2. **The operational policies a physical venue legally needs** do not exist.
3. **The network's own birth and death** (admission, wind-down, capital) are unspecified.
4. **The regeneration loop never closes** - impact is measured but carries no consequence.

Plus two lower-tier, more strategic absences (inter-hub relations; the knowledge commons).

**Severity key.** **High** = a real legal/operational exposure or a hole in the network's core purpose; address before scaling. **Medium** = a genuine subsystem gap, bounded in near-term risk. **Low / strategic** = completeness and movement-positioning, not near-term risk.

## 1. Participants and guests have no standing

### SG1. The people the hubs serve are absent from the architecture - High

The entire constellation governs **contributors**: members, stewards, mentors, partners, ventures, owners, elders. But a regenerative hub exists to serve someone - retreat guests, programme attendees, clients, the surrounding community, the land's human neighbours. These people appear only as **project codes in the Ledger** (GT-3.4, client-pseudonymisation) and as the object of the sliding-scale access rule (GT-3A.3). They have no charter, no voice, no feedback channel, no complaint path, and no recourse if harmed or mis-sold. For a network whose stated purpose (0.0 §1) is regeneration *of places and communities*, the beneficiary is structurally missing from its own governance.

*Fix direction:* a **participant / guest / community charter** (companion doc) - the rights of the served, a feedback and complaint route, consumer-protection basics for paid programmes, and the relationship between the network and the wider community it claims to regenerate. Distinct from membership: one can be served without contributing.

## 2. The operational policies a real venue needs

The Companion Docs folder holds Fund, Passport, Operational Procedures, RIF, LGR - and none of the three policies an APS/ETS running physical premises and hosting programmes is effectively required to hold. These are also what an insurer or the RUNTS will ask to see, so they are not optional polish.

### SG2. No safeguarding policy or code of conduct - High

Safeguarding today is only **reactive triggers**: A8 §8.4A (Elder safety suspension), A2 §7.1 (suspension pending allegation), A4 §8 (Mentor safeguarding). There is no positive system: vetting for those working with minors or vulnerable adults, a designated safeguarding lead, a disclosure and reporting procedure, a conduct code, how children at hubs are handled. Physical venues hosting retreats and programmes cannot run on suspension clauses alone.

*Fix direction:* a **Safeguarding Policy + Code of Conduct** companion doc; the existing suspension clauses become its enforcement limb rather than the whole of it.

### SG3. No coherent risk and insurance framework - High

Liability is scattered and inconsistent: A1 §3.3 names the Owner additional insured only "where possible"; the Art. 2053 c.c. owner-ruin liability tail is flagged but not closed (Balance Review A1-G4); the A2 §6 member cover has a gap during contribution-based stays (A2-G3); Phase-1 venture work sits awkwardly in "official volunteer activities" (A7-G4). No single instrument states what is insured, who holds the policies, public liability for events, professional indemnity for mentors, or what happens on an uninsured loss.

*Fix direction:* a **Risk & Insurance** companion doc consolidating the scattered per-agreement notes into one framework; resolves several Balance Review mediums at the system level rather than clause by clause.

## 3. The network's own birth and death

### SG4. No network-admission gate for a new node - Medium

A1 onboards an **Asset** (the §3.4 investment gate) and Hub DNA (A1-5) configures a hub, but nothing governs **admitting a new node to the network**: mission-fit screening, the network's capacity to actually support a new place, who decides, on what criteria. Onboarding an asset is not the same act as admitting a hub into the constellation.

*Fix direction:* a network-admission process (Operational Procedures or a short companion doc) - criteria, who approves (Board vs Assembly), and the capacity check before a new node is taken on.

### SG5. No wind-down protocol for the living system - Medium

Dissolution is handled **fiscally** (GT-13; Art. 9 CTS devoluzione of assets to the Third Sector). But there is no wind-down protocol for the network as a living system: what happens to in-flight ventures, resident stewards' housing, comodato assets mid-term, the common reservoir, and the Ledger / Passport data if the Association folds. Asset devolution is not the same as winding down people and relationships.

*Fix direction:* a wind-down / failure protocol covering the human and relational estate, not only the patrimony - sequencing, notice, data, and the duty of care owed to resident stewards and dependent ventures on dissolution.

### SG6. Acquisition is under-funded by construction - High

The path-to-commons designed in [[Regeneration Annex - Value Return on Sale - 2026-06-11]] (§9) and A1 §6.3A relies on the **Asset Reserve** (GT-4.4B, per-asset, non-overflowing) plus opportunistic donations. There is no **financing instrument** - no loan, social bond, capital campaign, or cross-holon pooling - and the common reservoir is explicitly for *operating* shortfalls, not capital (GT-4.8). For a real-property acquisition (often EUR 100k+), "topped up by Board allocation" (§6.3A(c)) names no actual source. The network can intend to buy a farmhouse into commons and have no way to fund it.

*Fix direction:* a capital / financing strategy - how the network funds large acquisitions and weathers capital (not operating) liquidity events; interacts with the F1 question on ETS borrowing and on the Asset Reserve as *riserva vincolata* (F1 Nota §9).

## 4. The regeneration loop never closes

### SG7. Impact is measured but carries no consequence - High

The Regenerative Impact Framework classifies impact across six dimensions (GT-1.6, GT-2.2). But there is **no consequence loop.** The homeostat re-tunes a holon on its *financial* balance (A3 §5A: measured balance initiates review, GT-4.8 graduated intervention). There is no equivalent for *regenerative* balance: if a hub persistently extracts, harms the land, or fails its impact, nothing in the architecture re-tunes, supports, or de-lists it. The network measures regeneration but does not act on the measurement.

*Fix direction:* an **impact-to-consequence loop** - a regenerative homeostat paralleling the financial one (re-tune first, recompose last), tying sustained RIF underperformance to support, review, and only at the last resort, hub-status consequences. This is the gap closest to the network's actual reason for existing.

## 5. Lower-tier / strategic

### SG8. Inter-hub relations and hub exit are unspecified - Medium

The model is hub <-> network (Association). Hub-to-hub is thin: mutual aid between hubs, inter-hub disputes, resource sharing, and a hub (or its owner) *leaving* the network. A1 termination covers the **asset** leaving; nothing speaks to the **community** around a departing hub, or to federation with other networks/commons.

*Fix direction:* an inter-hub coordination layer and a hub-exit protocol (community continuity distinct from asset return).

### SG9. The knowledge commons is undeclared - Strategic

The constellation is itself valuable IP. A7 §9 licenses *venture* IP to the Association, but the network's own playbook - the agreements, Hub DNA templates, the whole model - is neither declared open to the commons movement nor explicitly held proprietary. A values-relevant choice the constellation has not made.

*Fix direction:* a knowledge-commons / replication-licence position - whether and how other networks may adopt the model.

## Priority order

1. **SG2 + SG3** (safeguarding; risk & insurance) - the most urgent: a real legal/operational exposure before any hub hosts a programme.
2. **SG7** (the regeneration loop) - the most important: the missing teeth on the network's own purpose.
3. **SG1** (the served) - the biggest conceptual blind spot.
4. **SG6** (capital/financing) - blocks the path-to-commons already designed; partly F1-dependent.
5. **SG4 + SG5** (admission; wind-down) - the network's lifecycle ends.
6. **SG8 + SG9** - strategic, not near-term.
