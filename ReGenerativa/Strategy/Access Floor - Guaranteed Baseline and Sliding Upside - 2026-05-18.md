# Access Floor - Guaranteed Baseline and Sliding Upside

*Proposal only. Nothing in the vault changes until decided. Companion to the 2026-05-17
simplification ([[0.3 Decision Log]]). It addresses the one residual soft spot in that
work: the access benefit is currently clear but discretionary, so a counterparty cannot
point to anything they are guaranteed. This proposal adds a small, funded floor without
re-introducing tiers and without touching the eight agreements' structure.*

---

## The deal in one line

> **Every member in good standing has a guaranteed baseline of access (a right).
> Above that baseline, the existing sliding-scale rule (GT-3A.3) applies unchanged: the
> more you contribute, the higher your priority and the lower your price, up to free.**

Floor = a right you can point to in the contract. Upside = the generous discretion that
already exists. The simplification stays intact; only a signable baseline is added.

---

## The floor, split by what it actually costs

The floor is deliberately two tiers **by cost to provide**, because only one needs
money:

### Tier U — Unconditional (zero cash; promised flat)

Guaranteed to every member in good standing, with no judgement to claim and no funding
required:

- A **priority booking window**: members may book hub stays and programme places a
  stated number of days ahead of non-member/public access.
- **No member is turned away from a hub for inability to pay** a contribution-based
  stay where the hub has genuine spare capacity (this already lives in the Hub DNA
  spirit; the floor states it as a right).
- Recognition of contribution in the participant's Network Passport record (already
  true; restated as an entitlement).

These cost scheduling and goodwill, not cash — hubs are held under *comodato* (A1), so
the asset cost is already near zero. Tier U can be an **unconditional guarantee** today.

### Tier R — Reserve-scaled (the only cash part)

- **At least one free programme place per member per year**, network-wide, claimable
  first-come within a published window — **active only while the Network Regeneration
  Fund holds at least the published Floor Reserve** (see below).

Tier R is a real, recurring liability and is therefore **never a flat promise**. It
scales with the reserve behind it.

---

## Reserve-scaled funding (baked in)

Where the cash comes from, and why it cannot bankrupt anything:

1. **Most of the floor needs no money** (Tier U) — it is priority and
   non-exclusion-with-spare-capacity, not a transfer.
2. **The free programme place is largely cross-subsidised inside the activity** — the
   paying tiers of a programme fund its free place (the existing full-to-free model);
   the OQ4 gift-economy-downside decision already routes the residual gap. Net new cash
   need is only the shortfall.
3. **That shortfall is met from the existing machinery only**: the **Network
   Regeneration Fund** (GT-4: ≥5%, range 3–10%, of Net Revenue from Partnership,
   Intrapreneurship Phase 2/3, and revenue-generating Mentorship) and **Sponsorship**
   (Agreement 5). The €25 membership fee (A2) is *not* a funding source — it covers
   insurance.

**The Floor Reserve mechanism** (mirrors the LGR-2 Elder-Fund self-correcting logic, so
the network already knows this pattern):

- The Board publishes a **Floor Reserve** figure annually — the minimum NRF balance at
  which the Tier R guarantee is active.
- While `NRF balance ≥ Floor Reserve`: the free-place guarantee is live, at the
  published number of places.
- While `NRF balance < Floor Reserve`: Tier R is **suspended** (Tier U continues
  unconditionally); members are told plainly, and voluntary contributions may be
  invited — exactly the Care Protocol's below-reserve behaviour.
- **Ratchet:** the Board may widen the floor (more places, lower reserve trigger) at
  any time; it may **not** cut below a published level without a governance decision
  and notice. A guarantee cannot be quietly hollowed out.
- The number of free places scales with NRF health, never the reverse — the network
  promises only what its own economic activity can fund.

So the worst case is honest and bounded: in a lean year Tier R quietly pauses and Tier U
still holds. The network never owes a cheque it cannot cover. This is the Elder-Fund
lesson applied before the mistake, not after.

## Document footprint (if adopted — not done here)

Minimal, and it does not re-tier anything:

- One short clause in **GT-3A** (a "Guaranteed Access Floor" sub-clause: states Tier U
  as unconditional and Tier R as reserve-scaled, both as rights, with review under
  GT-7.2).
- A **Floor Schedule** recorded as a new governance resolution in
  [[Living Governance Resolutions]] (e.g. LGR-5): the Floor Reserve figure, the number
  of free places, the priority-window length — all Board-tunable upward, ratchet-
  protected. Same instrument class as LGR-2.
- One [[0.3 Decision Log]] entry.

No change to the eight agreements' bodies, no tiers, no perk table, one rule preserved.

---

## Honest costs and risks

- **Tier U is genuinely unconditional** — including an opportunity cost: a member
  priority-booking window means a hub in high demand sometimes turns away a
  higher-paying non-member to honour a member's window. That is the point of a floor;
  name it so it is chosen knowingly.
- **Tier R will feel thin when the network is poor.** That is correct behaviour, not a
  defect: a small honoured guarantee beats a large broken one. Accept that the floor
  grows with the network rather than leading it.
- **It is still a commitment.** The Board must actually publish and hold the Floor
  Reserve and report against it in the *bilancio sociale*, like the Elder Fund and Care
  reserve. A floor nobody funds or reports is worse than no floor.

---

## What it fixes

Converts the access benefit from "a steward may, in good faith…" into "**you are
guaranteed [Tier U] unconditionally, and [Tier R] whenever the network can fund it; and
beyond that the sliding scale rewards contribution.**" A counterparty can point to a
concrete right; the discretion is now only the upside, which is fine to be
discretionary. Newcomers get a real day-one baseline, which also softens the
rich-get-richer dynamic in the bare model.

---

## Decision needed

1. Adopt the floor (Tier U unconditional + Tier R reserve-scaled)?
2. If yes, set the **initial provisional figures** (owner/Board, revisable like any LGR):
   - Floor Reserve figure (the NRF balance that activates Tier R).
   - Number of free programme places per member per year at/above that reserve.
   - Priority-window length (days members book ahead of public).

On a yes, execution is ~2 edits + one Decision Log entry. Until then nothing in the
vault changes.
