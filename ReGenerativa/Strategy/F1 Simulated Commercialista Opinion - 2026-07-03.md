# F1 Simulated Commercialista Opinion

*A simulated advisor pass: the twelve questions of [[F1 Nota per il Commercialista - 2026-06-13]] answered as a Third-Sector commercialista would, each with a verdict and a suggested fix. Prepared by AI (RG S44) to sharpen the drafts and the real engagement — it is **not** a professional opinion, was reasoned from the CTS (D.Lgs. 117/2017) and general practice **without** the Statute or current Agenzia delle Entrate circulars, and **clears no (Pending F1) marker**. The formal F1 review governs and prevails. Builds on [[F1 Pre-Review - Fiscal Notes - 2026-06-10]] without repeating it.*

---

## Headline

The architecture is fiscally coherent, and its strongest feature is the thing that looks most exotic: the single-subject design. Because the Association is the only legal and fiscal person, the entire GT-4 machinery — NRC between internal holons, ARC, carried balances, reservoir transfers, Asset Reserve accrual — is management accounting, not transactions. No tax events hide inside it (Q1, Q6, Q8, Q9).

Two live exposures remain, both known and both manageable:

1. **The external NRC** (A6 Vehicle Entities, post-Emergence ventures) is a corrispettivo and must be run openly as one: invoiced, VAT-charged, counted against the DM 107/2021 ceilings, and backed by a defined service description. Verdict: workable, with drafting fixes (Q1).
2. **The Art. 17(3) access linkage** is defensible as a mutualistic access criterion but sits closest to the line of anything in the constellation. Verdict: defensible with the mitigations already drafted, plus three further softenings (Q3).

One clause needs rewriting regardless (GT-4.5(g), Q6b), one design decision needs the owner's eyes (Regeneration Annex: enforceability vs donor relief, Q7a), and the VAT-reform status must be the first item verified in the real engagement (Q5).

Consolidated fix list at the end (F-1 to F-12).

## Q1 — NRC qualification per payer type (the master question)

**Verdict: three classes, three treatments. The design survives; the drafting must stop using one word for two different things.**

**(i) Internal holons.** Hubs, projects, and ventures without their own legal personality are accounting partitions of one subject. A "payment" between them is a book entry. No supply exists for VAT purposes (VAT requires two persons; DPR 633/72 sees only one), no revenue or cost arises for IRES, nothing touches Art. 6 or Art. 79(5). Confirmed as the Nota assumes. Corollary: **never issue internal invoices**, not even pro-forma "for clarity" — a self-invoice between partitions is the one way to make this look like a transaction.

**(ii) A6 Vehicle Entities.** A contractually mandatory percentage of a separate legal person's revenue, paid to the Association in exchange for ecosystem benefits, is a **corrispettivo for services**. There is no serious alternative reading: compulsory payments are never liberal, and the benefits (marks, platform, network coordination, access to the constellation's frameworks) are real and identifiable. Consequences: *attività diverse* ex Art. 6 CTS, to be held secondary and instrumental within DM 107/2021 (≤30% of overall revenues or ≤66% of costs — early on, with membership fees and donations dominating the denominator, headroom should be comfortable, but it must be *measured*, see Q5); IVA at 22% with ordinary invoicing.

**(iii) Post-Emergence entities.** Same characterisation, with one extra weakness: a **perpetual** fee needs a perpetual service. If the emerged entity pays forever but the agreement never says what it keeps receiving, the fee is fragile both contractually and fiscally (an indeterminate perpetual corrispettivo invites scrutiny of what it really pays for). Define the continuing service basket — licence of marks, continued network membership and Passport recognition, access to demand signals and coordination — and the perpetuity becomes an ordinary evergreen services agreement terminable per its terms.

**Fixes (F-1):** in GT-4.4(a), A6 §10A, and A7-3, split the vocabulary: for any payer that is a separate legal person, the Regeneration Contribution **is invoiced by the Association as a fee for defined network services, plus VAT**, with the service description enumerated in the agreement; the "deduction at source" language applies to internal holons only. This also answers the **presentation question** (Nota Q8 note): internally, presenting the NRC as a cost-coverage line inside each holon's `C` is the cleaner management-accounting picture and is fiscally indifferent — keep GT-4.4(a)'s at-source framing if the model prefers it, it changes nothing; externally there is no choice, it is an invoice.

## Q2 — The money-to-access firewall (GT-3A.4A)

**Verdict: holds as drafted. Full exclusion was the right call; a reduced weighting would have shrunk the synallagma without removing it.**

Two conditions keep it holding:

1. **Practice must match text.** The clause is only as good as the stewards' behaviour. If donors de facto get priority, the firewall is pierced regardless of GT-3A.4A. Add one Op Proc line: allocation and access decisions are made without reference to the donor register, and the annual review spot-checks this.
2. **Liberality documentation** (F-10): all donations by traceable means (bonifico, card — a condition of Art. 83 relief); receipt letters stating *erogazione liberale, nessuna controprestazione*; in-kind donations valued per DM 28.11.2019 (independent appraisal above the thresholds); an annual donor summary. Recognition under A5 (public thanks, the memory of the gift in the ledger) is non-economic and safe; anything with market value (free nights, discounts, priority) is not recognition, it is consideration — the clause already says this; keep it policed.

## Q3 — Art. 17(3) CTS: volunteering vs the access rule

**Verdict: defensible as a mutualistic access criterion, with the drafted mitigations plus three further softenings. This is the constellation's closest-to-the-line mechanism and the one to present most carefully in the real F1.**

The reasoning a verifier would follow: volunteer work may not be remunerated in any way, even by the beneficiary; the ledger records mostly volunteer work; the sliding scale converts recorded contribution into economic benefit (down to gratuity). The defence: member access advantages set **discretionally**, on **plural criteria**, with **no conversion rate**, are mutualistic access policy (like seniority or need), not wages in kind. Everything already drafted pushes the right way: no tiers, no perk schedule, no hour-to-euro rate, good-faith steward discretion, contribution deliberately not reduced to hours, the discernment place and rotation in A4 §4.5.

**Further softenings (F-3):**

1. **Plural criteria in the clause itself.** GT-3A.3 should name contribution as *one signal among several* — alongside need, mission fit, and rotation — rather than the single axis the scale slides on. This is the single strongest drafting move available: it converts "score buys discount" into "stewards weigh several mutualistic factors, of which standing contribution is one".
2. **Gratuity as decision, not entitlement.** Free places are the risky end of the scale. Frame every free or below-cost place as a discretionary hospitality/bursary decision, recorded with a one-line reason — never as a level the scale "reaches".
3. **Language hygiene.** Nowhere in any document, form, or UI should the words *earn, redeem, credit, balance, spend* appear near access. The Passport shows contribution; it never shows a price.

Also keep the compliance floor tight, because it is the first thing checked in a verifica: registro dei volontari (Art. 17(1)), volunteer insurance (Art. 18), and clean role separation wherever a person is both volunteer and paid contractor (A3 §8.2 pattern). If the real advisor wants maximum safety they will propose cutting the ledger-access link entirely (pure discernment); the current design is one deliberate notch short of that and, with the softenings, defensible.

## Q4 — A6 §9.3 relational contribution share

**Verdict: the four conditions are necessary and nearly sufficient. Add two.**

The four (ex-ante slice, written criteria, full invoicing, role separation) close the retroactive-pot pattern. Remaining gap: nothing bounds the **size** of the slice, and an oversized "relational" share to a related party (or a partner in regime forfettario) re-opens the income-shifting reading through the front door.

**Fixes (F-4):** (5) a proportionality ceiling fixed ex ante — the Project Sheet states the slice as a percentage, with a network default ceiling held in an LGR (suggest ≤15% of the distributable) that a Project Sheet may only exceed with a recorded justification; (6) the recipient's invoices describe the actual service (coordination, facilitation, relational stewardship) at normal value — generic "contribution share" line items are what turn a legitimate service fee into an allocation of someone else's income.

## Q5 — VAT reform status and Art. 79(5) monitoring

**Verdict: assume the reform is in force; verify first thing in the real engagement.**

The repeal of the *fuori campo IVA* treatment of member corrispettivi (D.L. 146/2021, Art. 5(15-quater)) was last verified as deferred to 1 January 2026; absent a further eleventh-hour deferral, it now applies. If in force: member corrispettivi (the A4 resource-cost band above all) shift from out-of-scope to **VAT-exempt with obligations**. Operationally (F-11): the Association needs a partita IVA in any case (the external NRC of Q1 is taxable); take the **dispensa ex Art. 36-bis DPR 633/72** for exempt operations (it removes invoicing/registration burden on the exempt side at the cost of input-VAT deduction, which is largely nil here); keep taxable streams (external NRC, market-rate band to the public) segregated in the accounts, since mixed activity triggers pro-rata mechanics.

**The Art. 79(5) dashboard (F-6)** — build it as a standing quarterly table, not a year-end discovery. Classification of each stream:

| Stream | Side of the test |
|---|---|
| Membership fees (A2) | non-commercial |
| Donations, restricted gifts, legacies (A5, Q12) | non-commercial |
| Occasional public fundraising (Art. 79(4)) | non-commercial |
| Resource-cost band to members (A4, Art. 85) | non-commercial (verify against Statute) |
| Market-rate band to the public (A4) | commercial |
| External NRC / network-services fees (Q1) | commercial |
| A6 partnership flows received by the Association | commercial |
| Internal NRC, ARC, reserve accrual, reservoir transfers | neither — internal, never in the ratio |

Board reviews the ratio quarterly; alert threshold at 25% commercial (headroom before the 30% DM 107/2021 ceiling); annual confirmation in the relazione di missione. Note Art. 79(5-bis/ter) tolerance margins soften a one-off breach, but the dashboard is what keeps it one-off.

## Q6 — GT-4 as a whole

**(a) Hidden tax events in internal flows: none** — with three bookkeeping cautions. (i) The holon ledger is contabilità gestionale; the statutory bilancio (DM 5.3.2020 models) remains unitary, and every internal transfer must net to zero in it — carried balances are memoranda, never receivables/payables. (ii) The moment a flow crosses to a **separate legal person** (post-Emergence entity, federation partner under GT-4.10(b)) it stops being internal — the Q1 regime applies. (iii) The word **"mutual insurance"** (GT-4.8(d)) is safe as an internal metaphor but must never describe a promise to an external entity — insurance is a regulated activity. Where post-Emergence or federated entities can see the reservoir's support, rename it *discretionary solidarity support* (F-9).

**(b) GT-4.5(g) enumeration — the flagged risk is real; rewrite (F-2).** The current list ("personal income, salary, pension, or profit distribution") reads as exhaustive, and Art. 8(2)-(3) CTS is deliberately broader (it presumes indirect distribution in above-market pay, below-market sales to members, excessive interest, and a residual "any case" clause). Suggested wording: *"no flow at any scale constitutes, directly or indirectly, a distribution of profits or assets to any person — including, without limitation, personal income, salary, pension, profit distribution, or any form presumed indirect distribution under Art. 8(2)-(3) CTS."* One clause, closes the gap, and gives every future drafter the Art. 8(3) presumption list as a checklist.

**(c) A3 §9.3 60-day reimbursements: legitimate and good practice** — provided reimbursements are analytical and documented (Art. 17(3)). Self-certified expenses only within the Art. 17(4) bounds (max €10/day, €150/month, against a prior Board deliberation), and **no flat-rate reimbursements ever** — a forfait reimbursement to a volunteer is remuneration. One sentence in A3 §9 or Op Proc Section on reimbursements (F-5).

**(d) LGR delegation (Block 2(c)):** fiscally acceptable — parameters as Board resolutions are exactly how an ETS should hold revisable figures. The one fiscal constraint: any rate that determines an **invoiced external fee** (the external NRC) must be published and uniform, which LGR-2 already ensures. Contract-law admissibility of the delegation is the lawyer's half.

## Q7 — Regeneration Annex (value return on sale)

**Verdict: structurally sound, but it contains a genuine either/or the owner must decide: enforceability or donor relief — not both (F-7).**

**(a) The owner-to-Association payment.** If the value-return is a contractual obligation (a *modus* on the comodato), its payment is performance of an obligation, **not** an *erogazione liberale*: no Art. 83 relief for the owner. On the Association's side it is a non-commercial patrimonial receipt (no service is rendered for it) — outside IRES commercial income, outside VAT — which is the good half. The alternative (a non-binding moral commitment, honoured by a spontaneous donation at sale) preserves Art. 83 for the owner but is unenforceable. **Recommendation:** keep it enforceable, say plainly in the Annex that the mandatory share carries no deduction, and add an express option for the owner to top up voluntarily at sale — the top-up, being spontaneous, is a true liberalità with Art. 83 relief. Both flows stay clean because they are never mixed.

**(b) Ipoteca in favour of an ETS.** Registration of a voluntary hypothec securing the value-return: the ordinary regime is 2% ipotecaria on the secured amount; Art. 82 CTS fixed-rate/exemption treatment plausibly applies to an ETS non commerciale, but the exact line item (Art. 82(3) vs (5), and whether iscrizione of security qualifies) **must be verified with the notary and the real advisor** — do not assume the €200 fixed rate in the financial model. Bollo exemption (Art. 82(5)) applies either way. The massimale-revision mechanics are notarial, not fiscal.

**(c) Recharacterisation of the comodato.** The mitigants to draft in: the burden is **contingent** (only on sale), **residual** (only on appreciation above the OMI benchmark), **conditioned on the network's own investment** (only above the threshold), and the comodato carries **no periodic payment** of any kind. Add one express clause: *the modus does not constitute consideration for the enjoyment of the Asset, which remains gratuitous*. Doctrine helps here: a modus that does not absorb the value of the gratuitous benefit leaves the gratuitous cause prevailing. Keep the burden's expected value demonstrably below a market rent for the same period and the registro/locatizia recharacterisation argument loses its footing. (Shared with the lawyer, as the Nota notes.)

## Q8 — ARC (GT-4.9)

**Verdict: (a) confirmed, (b) confirmed, (c) the analysis improves, not worsens.**

(a) Between holons of the same subject the ARC is the same nothing-event as the internal NRC — no supply, no VAT, no invoice (see the Q1 corollary: no internal invoicing, ever). (b) It never enters the Art. 79(5) ratio or the DM 107/2021 ceilings, because it is not revenue — it is an allocation of revenue already earned and already classified at the edge where it entered (a member's programme fee stays decommercialised; a public ticket stays commercial; the ARC slice moving to the Asset holon changes neither). (c) Removing the asset-hour line from the resource-cost place price moves that price *closer* to direct-delivery cost. Art. 85 decommercialisation does not depend on the price's internal composition — it depends on who pays (members), for what (direct implementation of institutional purpose), and per the Statute; a lower, still-honest cost price disturbs nothing. The discipline to keep: the documented cost composition (A4 §4.2) stays transparent, and the market-rate band keeps carrying full costs so its commercial margin is what it claims to be.

The NRC presentation question attached to this item is answered under Q1 (F-1): internally indifferent, externally an invoice.

## Q9 — Asset Reserve (GT-4.4B)

**Verdict: correct frame is a *riserva vincolata* in patrimonio netto, destined by the organi sociali — not an accantonamento a fondo. Admissible, with a documentation discipline.**

(a) An accantonamento (fondo oneri) requires a present obligation toward a third party; an intention to buy or replace an asset is not one. The right home is **patrimonio netto → riserve vincolate per decisione degli organi istituzionali** (modello A, DM 5.3.2020), constituted by destination of avanzi. Perfectly admissible for an ETS: the non-distribution constraint governs *destination*, not *accumulation*, and accruing toward an instrumental asset is a paradigmatic institutional use of patrimonio (Art. 8(1)). If a slice of the reserve is ever funded by purpose-restricted gifts, that slice belongs in the parallel line **riserve vincolate destinate da terzi** (see Q12d).

(b) Bilancio: a distinct line in patrimonio netto per the model; the relazione di missione discloses purpose, target `R`, the year's movements, and the acquisition/replacement plan; mirrored in the bilancio sociale when the Art. 14 threshold (€1M entrate) is ever crossed.

(c) No effect on Art. 79(5) or DM 107/2021 — those tests read flows, not reserves. The one genuine caution: a large avanzo accumulating year over year invites the "effective pursuit of purpose" question in a verifica. The antidote is the plan: a Board-deliberated destination, a named Asset, a target figure, a horizon (F-8 checklist).

(d) Multi-year carry is fine. Renew the destination by Board deliberation annually (a one-line resolution), never book it as a liability, and the GT-4.4B(f) one-way ratchet is itself evidence of institutional destination — cite it in the relazione.

## Q10 — Acquisition and the path to commons (A1 §6.3A)

**(a) Purchase.** The governing relief is **Art. 82(4) CTS**: registro, ipotecaria, and catastale in fixed measure for onerous acquisitions by an ETS non commerciale, **on condition** that the deed declares the property will be directly used for institutional activities and that use begins within five years (failing which: ordinary tax, a 30% surcharge, and interest). Ordinary regime if the condition fails: 9% registro. **Fix (F-8):** an acquisition checklist — deed declaration, five-year direct-use plan attached to the Board file, and the Statute checked for whether immovable acquisitions need Assembly (not only Board) authorisation (the lawyer's Block 3.6 point; the fiscal relief does not cure a governance defect).

**(b) Donation route.** Gratuitous transfers to an ETS are exempt from donation tax and from ipotecaria/catastale (**Art. 82(2)**); notarial deed required for immovables. The donor takes Art. 83 relief on an in-kind donation valued per DM 28.11.2019 (independent appraisal for property). No indirect-distribution profile — the flow runs toward the Association.

**(c) Valuation method for Annex 1.** Fix it ex ante and make it determinable without fresh negotiation: an independent sworn appraisal (perizia asseverata) by a valuer appointed per an agreed mechanism, anchored to OMI ranges, **net of network-funded improvements** (the Regeneration Annex §9 axis, so the network does not buy back its own investment). The fiscal constraint that matters: **cap the price at the appraised value**. Paying a member-Owner above fair value is the mirror image of the Art. 8(3) below-market-sale presumption — it would be read as indirect distribution dressed as a purchase.

**(d) Recharacterisation via option/ROFR.** Fiscal side of the shared question: keep the option **opt-in for the Owner, at fair value determined at exercise** — which A1 §6.3A already does. What would poison the comodato is a locked below-market price conceded in exchange for the loan; a fair-value option concedes nothing and prices nothing. (Remainder with the lawyer.)

## Q11 — Minor flags

**Ledger visibility:** no fiscal-documentation issue. The ledger is gestionale; the fiscal record is the invoice/receipt pipeline (GT-12). One cheap discipline: at each settlement, reconcile the €-valued distribution entries in project/venture partitions against the invoices actually issued — divergence between the visible ledger and the fiscal record is the kind of inconsistency a verifica enjoys. **Client pseudonymisation:** fine — invoices carry legal names; the ledger's project codes have no fiscal role.

## Q12 — Capital instruments for acquisition

**(a) Debt capacity.** An APS-ETS can borrow; there is no CTS prohibition. Constraints are practical and governance-side: banks lend against bilanci and cash flow; mortgages only on **owned** property (a comodatario cannot hypothecate the Owner's asset — for comodato assets the only securable value is the Q7(b) mechanism on the Owner's side); immovable-related borrowing is straordinaria amministrazione — check the Statute for Assembly authorisation. One hard fiscal rail (F-12): interest paid to anyone other than banks/authorised intermediaries above **4 points over the reference rate is presumed indirect distribution (Art. 8(3))** — every mission-aligned or community loan must be capped under that line.

**(b) Community capital.** Equity is structurally unavailable (no shares in an association; non-distribution). **Member loans** are possible within guardrails: members only, no public solicitation, non-transferable instruments (staying clear of raccolta di risparmio tra il pubblico, Art. 11 TUB), written terms, interest under the Art. 8(3) cap. The cooperative *prestito sociale* regime is coop-specific and not available to an APS. **Titoli di solidarietà (Art. 77 CTS)** — bank-issued instruments whose proceeds fund ETS — are the institutional-grade route: worth a conversation with a candidate partner bank once the acquisition fund is real.

**(c) Crowdfunding restricted to acquisition.** Donation-based: erogazioni liberali (Art. 83 relief if traceable), and occasional public campaigns sit in Art. 79(4) raccolta fondi — the safe default. Reward-based: material rewards make the receipts corrispettivi (commercial, VAT) — avoid, or keep rewards strictly symbolic (recognition, per the A5/GT-3A.4A grammar the constellation already has). Lending-based only via authorised platforms and under the (a)/(b) rails. The purpose restriction itself is fine — a donazione modale, with the A5 §3.3 consult-and-return fallback supporting rather than undermining liberality (return conditioned on purpose failure does not create a counter-performance).

**(d) Network-level acquisition fund.** Same frame as Q9, one level up: a riserva vincolata in patrimonio netto, reported in **two lines** — the slice destined by the organi (Board-destined avanzi) and the slice destined by third parties (restricted gifts and legacies; legacies also exempt under Art. 82(2)). Keep the two provenances separate in the accounts from day one; they carry different reversibility (the Board can re-destine its own slice by deliberation, never the donors'). A formal patrimonio destinato (Art. 10 CTS) is available but adds machinery the reserve lines already deliver — not recommended at this scale.

**(e) Art. 79(5) impact.** Donations, legacies, and occasional fundraising land on the **non-commercial** side and strengthen the ratio; debt is not revenue at all. The only variant that would burden the commercial side is reward-based crowdfunding — one more reason for (c)'s default.
