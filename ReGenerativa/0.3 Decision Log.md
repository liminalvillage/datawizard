## 2026-06-15 — Asset Reserve + network-as-holon unification (GT-4.4B, GT-4.4C, A1 §6.3A)

**Decision.** Building on the ARC entry below, two structural moves, all *(Pending F1)*:

- **Asset Reserve (GT-4.4B).** Each Asset holon may accumulate a protected capital reserve toward **replacing or acquiring** its Asset. Corrected cascade (the earlier "fold replacement into `C`" idea was rejected — owner spotted that `D = α·C` would inflate the Steward's discretionary envelope and a provision is saved, not spent): the reserve is a **priority claim between cost-coverage (GT-4.4(b)) and discretionary (GT-4.4(c))**, filling to target `R` (= replacement cost, per asset, LGR-2). `D` stays sized on operating `C` only, so the Steward's envelope never grows with the reserve and **cannot reach it** — protection is *structural* (position in the shape), not a behavioural clause. The reserve is a distinct line (not the operating carried balance, never raided for shortfalls), per-asset, **portable** to a replacement asset, deployed by **Steward proposal + Board approval**, and a **one-way ratchet** into complete commons (non-distribution + devoluzione, GT-13/Art.9 CTS). Self-similar: `R = 0` for holons with no replaceable capital.
- **Network-as-holon unification (GT-4.4C).** The Association holon is recognised as an **ordinary holon**: cost-coverage `C` = network ops (`K_a`), discretionary = Board allocation, **non-terminal** so surplus overflows to the Elder Council — the network **cannot hoard** (an over-set contribution rate spills to the common reservoir, not the centre). The **Board (Consiglio Direttivo) is the network holon's Steward**, bound by the same GT-4.5 rails. **NRC and ARC are named as the network-scope and asset-scope of one Regeneration Contribution** ("use a shared holon's resource → contribute to its regeneration").
- **A1 §6.3A — Network Acquisition & Path to Commons.** Owner grants the network a right of first refusal (if they ever sell), an opt-in standing purchase option at a pre-agreed valuation (Annex 1), and a donation route (A5); acquisition funded from the Asset Reserve + Board top-up. Records the shared intention that a lent asset may become permanent commons.

**Considered and rejected.** Fold-replacement-into-`C` (breaks `D=α·C`); reserve as a separate `R`+`ρ` ratio with parametric overflow (more knobs than needed — collapsed to a single priority claim with target `R`, default fill = "before discretionary"); renaming the common reservoir → "Network Reserve" (rejected: 58 occurrences/18 files incl. a titled doc; would falsely imply NRC feeds it; over-unifies two different jobs — kept "common reservoir", named the new per-asset pool **Asset Reserve**); pooled acquisition fund (rejected earlier — per-asset chosen). **F1 reservation:** whether NRC is *presented* as an at-source deduction (today) or recast as a cost-coverage line in each holon's `C` is a fiscal-presentation question left to F1; its purpose is settled. Also for F1: Asset Reserve as *riserva vincolata* (owned) vs *accantonamento* (comodato); valuation method in §6.3A(b); whether real-property acquisition needs Assembly (not only Board) authorization under the Statute.

**Downstream.** GT → v3.26 (GT-4.4B, GT-4.4C; GT-4.4(a)/4.9 cross-refs), A1 → v3.12 (§6.3A), LGR → v1.14 (`R` parameter); Cheatsheet (GT-4 row + Key Numbers `R`), signer's FAQ (Owner path-to-commons Q), **The Fund in Plain Language** (plain-language: two refinements, "the network is a place too", "from borrowed to owned"). Plan: ~/.claude/plans/explain-what-would-happen-shimmying-dongarra.md.

## 2026-06-15 — Asset Regeneration Contribution (ARC): sharing back from entrusted Assets (GT-4.9)

**Decision.** A new value-flow rule so that monetizing on an Asset entrusted gratuitously/at cost (Agreement 1) shares back into regenerating that Asset, rather than the operator privatizing the whole surplus. Owner's principle: *it is not fair to monetize on an asset given at cost without sharing back.* All new text *(Pending F1)*.

- **GT-4.9 — Asset Regeneration Contribution (ARC).** General, self-executing: where any holon makes Net Revenue from activity using an A1-entrusted Asset, a single ARC (rate `r_ARC`, floor `r_ARC_min`, LGR-9) is taken **after the NRC, before any operator/participant distribution**, and routed to the **host Asset holon**, where the standard GT-4.4 shape covers the Asset's cost first and regenerates it with the remainder. NRC feeds the network; ARC feeds the Asset. Override only with the host Steward's written agreement, never below the floor; GT-11.7 if operator = Steward. Scope: A1-entrusted Assets only; at-cost activity (incl. A4 Resource-cost band) carries no ARC. One-line pointer added in GT-4.4(a).
- **LGR-9.** `r_ARC` provisional **25%**, `r_ARC_min` provisional **10%**, on Net Revenue after NRC. Placeholders pending owner/Board ratification.
- **Applications (pointers, no bespoke mechanism):** A4 §5.3A; A6 §10A.3 (+§10.1 flow); A7 §7.3; A3 §6.2.
- **Simplification unlocked (one-share model).** Because ARC lands in the Asset holon's cost-coverage bucket (GT-4.4(b)), A4 §5.2's "hub operational contribution" and "steward and organisational costs" lines were redundant and removed; "hub usage per hour" removed from the Resource-cost composition (§4.1/§4.2) — the Asset's standing cost is now carried holon-side, so gift slots cover only consumables (a deliberate deepening of the gift economy: high contributors get Asset use as part of earned access).

**Considered and rejected.** (1) *Two flows* — keep a separate per-band hub cost-recovery line plus a surplus share. Rejected as duplicating the holon's own cost-coverage bucket; the one-share model is simpler and self-similar. (2) *A4-only fix* — rejected: the unfairness is general (any operator on any gifted Asset), so the rule belongs in the GT with agreements pointing to it. (3) *Override to zero* — rejected: a non-zero floor guarantees sharing back always occurs. (4) Name "ARS" rejected (homophone); "Asset Regeneration Contribution (ARC)" chosen to parallel the NRC. Naming/model settled with the owner in chat.

**Downstream.** GT → v3.25, A3 → v3.18, A4 → v3.9, A6 → v3.8, A7 → v1.12, LGR → v1.13; Cheatsheet (GT-4 row + Key Numbers ARC row), signer's FAQ (NRC/ARC + "what does the hub get?"), Balance Review (2026-06-15 addendum). **F1 agenda:** ratify `r_ARC`/`r_ARC_min`; confirm the ARC is an internal allocation between holons of one legal entity (the Association is fiscal host for all holons, GT-1.10), not a separate taxable supply; confirm the resource-cost simplification doesn't disturb the Art. 85 CTS member-decommercialisation analysis.

**Considered and rejected.** The original request was vetoes *without giving any reason* and with no review route. **Downstream.** A1 → v3.10, A3 → v3.16; Cheatsheet (access-rule section gains the two-vetoes note); signer's FAQ (new Q&As in A1, A2, A3 sections). **F1 agenda:** whether member exclusion from association activities at a specific asset, even reasoned and reviewable, needs further framing under the Third Sector Code's participation rights.

**Addendum 2026-06-14 — Owner's Designated Access (A1 §2.8).** The mirror of the exclusion right added at the owner's request: the Owner may name an individual who **must** be granted access to the Asset, overriding a Steward's §4.4 veto. Bounded by safety, the Asset's carrying capacity, the Asset DNA (GT-1.5), and applicable law; cannot displace a booked participant or Steward operational need; confers no role/commercial benefit and doesn't waive a Programme's delivery cost (mirrors §2.5). A3 §4.4 made subordinate to both A1 §2.7 and §2.8; A1 §4.1 interference carve-out extended to §2.8. **Rationale:** completes the owner's control over who is and isn't on their property in both directions; the safety/DNA limits keep the network's duty of care intact even against an owner's instruction. **Downstream.** A1 → v3.11, A3 → v3.17; Cheatsheet and FAQ synced. **F1 agenda:** same participation-rights review now also covers a positive access mandate that could conflict with a Steward's safety judgement — the §2.8 safety/capacity carve-out is the intended resolution but should be confirmed.

## 2026-06-11 — Regeneration Annex: obligatory value return on sale, investment-gated at asset onboarding

**Decision.** The constellation gains an obligatory mechanism returning network-created asset value on sale, designed in [[Regeneration Annex - Value Return on Sale - 2026-06-11]] (architecture approved by owner; clause drafting pends F1 review of the note's §8 questions). Binary onboarding: assets *lent as-is* carry nothing new; the moment **planned network investment crosses the Annex 4 threshold** — before the first euro is spent, reusing the A1 §3.4 gate — a single **Regeneration Annex** is signed containing baseline value + OMI zone, investment/amortisation schedule, **regeneration payout** (share of `sale price − baseline × OMI appreciation`, with a 5-year tapering post-termination clawback), **ROFR to the Association at formula price**, payment-at-closing + clausola penale, and (real property only) **ipoteca volontaria** capped at the investment schedule × 150–200%, revised at each schedule update. Money flows Owner → Association/common reservoir, never to stewards personally. Placement: inside A1 framed as restitution of network-created value (extending the existing §3.4/Annex 4 causa), with an Art. 1424 conversion clause degrading gracefully to a side contract if the comodato's gratuitousness is threatened.

**Why.** The balance analysis identified the deepest unresolved extraction: network labour and money capitalise into the Owner's private asset, and sale captures the uplift privately. Voluntary instruments fail by adverse selection ("the owner should be obliged to pay or he won't do it" — owner direction); recognition-only returns have no teeth on a seller. Gating at onboarding puts the obligation at the moment of maximum fairness and leverage — the Owner is asking the network to spend on their property — and makes it self-enforcing via the existing no-spend-until-annex-complete rule.

**Considered and rejected:** steward-personal ROFR (unexercisable; obligatory effect only); euro payout to stewards prorata (Art. 8 CTS indirect-distribution risk via the Association, incentive distortion toward property-value maximisation, allocation disputes across rotating stewards); voluntary opt-in covenant (adverse selection); standalone side contract as primary form (superseded by in-A1 placement + conversion clause); a middle tier without ipoteca (merged — any above-threshold investment arms the full package, per owner direction). A recognition rider survives as garnish: the natural-person Owner's amount-blind Passport event and Owner→Elder track (strategy note §7).

**What changed:** strategy note created; MOC Strategy row added (and the agreements version table refreshed against frontmatter — it had drifted to the 06-08 state). No agreement text touched yet; F1 headline question is comodato modale compatibility.

## 2026-06-10 — Ledger unification: one record, partitioned by holon, all visible to all

**Decision.** The three contribution-recording structures — the network Contribution Ledger (GT-1.7), the Network Passport (GT-3A), and the Partnership Contribution Log (A6-4, also used by A7 ventures) — are unified as **one Ledger, partitioned by holon** (each Hub/Asset, Project, Venture, the Elder Council, and the Association holon holds its own partition), with **every partition visible to all network participants** (owner direction). The Passport was already defined as the per-person reading of the Ledger (GT-3A.1); the change formalises the A6-4 log as the Project partition of the same record rather than a parallel system, and removes the "commercially sensitive amounts" carve-out in favour of full visibility.

**The three readings of the one record:** access (lifetime-cumulative, portable, rankless — GT-3A.3); allocation (active/recent, local — GT-4.3); and now **distribution** (€-valued entries within a Project/Venture partition computing revenue shares — A6 §9–§10, A7 §7). New firewall in **GT-3A.4**: the euro valuations of distribution entries carry no weight in the access measure — entries count toward access as contributions of their kind, never at monetary value (same principle as GT-3A.4A; protects the Art. 17(3) CTS position flagged in [[F1 Pre-Review - Fiscal Notes - 2026-06-10]]).

**Only two things stay off-ledger:** personal-identity mappings (GDPR, GT-3.4/GT-5) and external client identities (project codes unless the client consents — third parties never signed the constellation). Entries published in a partition are expressly not Confidential Information under A6 §14.

**Why now:** the digital infrastructure (GT-3.1) is not yet built; unifying on paper first means building one system, not two needing reconciliation. A6's own definitions previously created a parallel "Contribution Ledger" term — a latent ambiguity now closed.

**Considered and rejected:** a restricted visibility layer for distribution amounts (rejected by owner — transparency is the principle, A6 §4); routing distribution € values into access scoring (rejected — fiscal firewall).

**What changed:** GT 3.20 → 3.21 (GT-1.7, GT-3A.4); A6 3.4 → 3.5 (§2 definition); A6-4 2.0 → 2.1 (§2 Form, §3 valuations note, §5 visibility); Op Procedures 1.8 (§K.1/K.2); Network Passport doc 3.2 → 3.3 ("One ledger, three readings"); 0.0 key concepts row; Cheatsheet GT-3 row; signer's FAQ synced. All *(Pending F1)*. F1 addition: confirm full visibility of distribution amounts is compatible with competition/antitrust sensitivity between Partner entities, and the client-pseudonymisation rule.

**Addendum (same day, owner direction): visibility made tunable.** Full visibility is the *current setting*, not a hard-coded rule: GT-1.7 now holds partition visibility as a Living Governance Resolution (**new LGR-8**), tunable prospectively by Board resolution, with an untunable floor — every participant sees their own entries, every member of a holon (including every Partner on a Project) sees that holon's partition in full, the Association as fiscal host sees all. This lets the network respond to scale, competition sensitivity, or F1 advice without re-signing agreements. ## 2026-06-02 — Allocation value systems: each holon declares how its Ledger becomes shares (pending F1)

**Decision.** Specified the missing function in contribution-weighted allocation: a holon's discretionary-envelope shares are computed by applying the holon's **declared value system** to its Contribution Ledger entries (GT-1.7, GT-1.8). The Ledger records *what* was contributed; the value system sets *how* it is weighted. Each holon declares its own system **independently** as a **weighted combination of indicators** — anything it can measure or signal: validated hours, euros, declared roles, mutual-credit units of valued activity (e.g. trees planted, meals cooked), or qualitative collaboration signals, in whatever mix and weighting it chooses. Common configurations are egalitarian, hour-based, or role-based (which avoids continuous accounting). Network default absent a declaration: **egalitarian** among active co-Stewards.

**Alternatives considered and rejected:**

- **Renumbering only A6 (where the gaps are more visible) and leaving A1's single gap.** Rejected. Partial normalisation creates a worse problem than no normalisation: a reader sees A6 is contiguous but A1 is not, and concludes that the A1 gap is meaningful when it is simply unaddressed.

## 2026-05-10 — Membership becomes A2; Stewardship becomes A3

**Decision:** Renumber the constellation so that Membership is Agreement 2 and Stewardship is Agreement 3. New order: A1 Ownership → A2 Membership → A3 Stewardship → A4 Mentorship → A5 Sponsorship → A6 Partnership → A7 Intrapreneurship → A8 Eldership.

**Decided by:** Holons (vault owner) in session 2026-05-10. Executed by Claude under DataWizard protocol.

**Context.** Until this decision, Membership was Agreement 3 and Stewardship was Agreement 2. The Stewardship-before-Membership ordering had reflected an older architectural lens in which the operational layer (the steward who holds a hub) was foregrounded over the gateway layer (the member who joins). In practice — confirmed by Agreement 3's own Clause 1.4 ("Membership is a prerequisite for natural persons holding any active role in the ReGenerativa constellation") and by GT-11.4 — Membership is the gateway. No natural person can hold any other active agreement without first being a Member. The constellation's own logic placed Membership before Stewardship; the numbering did not.

**Rationale.** Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **Numbering should reflect the prerequisite chain.** Membership is the threshold every natural person crosses first. Stewardship is one of several roles that becomes available once that threshold has been crossed. A reader encountering the agreements in order should encounter the gateway before encountering the role.

2. **The Architecture of Belonging cascade reads more truly with the new order.** The Introduction's "From X comes Y" narrative arc — place → belonging → presence → expertise → support → collaboration → creation — is the deepening sequence the network actually follows. A person finds the place, chooses to belong, may then deepen into stewardship, etc. The previous numbering forced the cascade to invert at the second step (place → presence → belonging) which read as a logical jolt rather than a deepening.

3. **The numbering creates legibility for new readers.** A first-time reader of the constellation should be able to learn the architecture from the order itself. Membership (A2) before Stewardship (A3) makes the gateway prerequisite immediately visible. Stewardship (A2) before Membership (A3) required a separate explanation.

**Alternatives considered and rejected:**

- **Leaving the numbering as-is and adding more cross-references to clarify the prerequisite.** Rejected. The constellation already had several places (GT-11.4, A3 Clause 1.4, Cheatsheet "Prerequisite chain" line) explaining that Membership came before Stewardship logically. Adding more such notes would treat the symptom, not the cause. Numbering carries semantic weight; if the numbering misleads, the cure is to fix the numbering.

- **Inserting Membership as A0 or A1.5 to preserve all other numbers.** Rejected. Non-standard numbering creates more friction than it saves. A clean integer sequence from 1 to 8 is the most legible option for legal documents that will eventually be signed and filed by counterparties unfamiliar with the constellation's internal history.

- **Performing the swap in a future cleanup pass rather than now.** Rejected. The longer the inverted numbering persists, the more downstream artefacts (signed agreements, decision references, external citations) will calcify around it. None of those exist yet at scale; the cost of swapping is finite and bounded today.

**Consequences executed in the same session:**

- Two file renames (with intermediate temporary names to avoid filename collisions on Obsidian's filesystem)
- Two annex renames following the same pattern (A2-1 Steward Onboarding becomes A3-1 Steward Onboarding; A3-1 Member Onboarding becomes A2-1 Member Onboarding)
- Frontmatter `parent_agreement` and `title` updates on both renamed annexes
- Body cross-reference sweep across:
  - All 8 agreements (A2-A3 wikilinks, parenthetical "(Agreement N)" references in A8)
  - 4 affected annexes (the two renamed onboarding annexes, plus A1-3 Hub Care Protocol and A1-6 Hub DNA which reference Stewardship)
  - General Terms GT-11.3 constellation list (reordered)
  - Constellation Cheatsheet (Eight Agreements table, Prerequisite chain note, Key Numbers table, Annexes-at-a-Glance table)
  - Introduction narrative (Architecture of Belonging cascade rewritten in new order; "Each part sustains the whole" sentences rewritten to match)
