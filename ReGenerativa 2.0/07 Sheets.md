# Sheets

*The three fill-in documents of ReGenerativa 2.0. Each is attached to the agreement it serves and may be updated by the people who signed it, in writing, without re-signing the agreement. Everything not on a sheet is in the [[02 Rulebook]].*

---

## Resource Sheet

*Attached to [[03 Agreement - Lending a Resource]] and to each [[05 Agreement - Caring for a Resource]] for the resource. One sheet serves both a place and an asset; skip what does not apply. Half a page to a page. Written by the owner and the first steward together.*

**The resource.** Name; whether it is a place or an asset; its address, or where it is kept; what it is (for a place: house, land, workshop; for an asset: vehicle, tool, equipment); what it includes and what it does not; its condition on the day it is lent; and how the network gets in or takes it out (keys, access, hours, booking).

**What it is for.** The purposes the resource may serve, and any it may not. For a place: who may stay, and any conditions the owner sets (for example a stricter share of members in residence than Rulebook 2.3, or a person excluded, with reasons). For an asset: who may operate it, and any licence or training required.

**Its rhythm.** For a place: the cycle that counts as residence (a month unless stated; a season if the place lives by seasons); when it rests; what the seasons ask of it. For an asset: when it is serviced, and when it rests.

**Caring for it.** The five things that keep it sound (for a house: roof and water, heating, waste, fire safety, cleaning between stays; for land: water, soil, fences, fire, animals; for a vehicle or tool: servicing, storage, insurance and licences, safe operation, cleaning between uses). Who to call. What the owner keeps for themselves (structural works, major overhauls) and what the network carries.

**Its two promises.** In a few lines, what "more alive" means for this resource. For a place: the state of its soil, water, buildings, people and culture today, and what the stewards intend to improve first. For an asset: what good care and wide use mean for it, and who it should reach. Reviewed each season against the [[Regenerative Impact Framework]].

**Its money.** The resource's monthly costs target (what it takes to keep it running); what a stay (for a place) or a use (for an asset) contributes to those costs, in money or in work, and how it slides with contribution (Rulebook 5.3, 5.4); its reserve target (replacement cost, or zero); whether the owner allows the reserve to co-fund structural works or major overhauls and on what repayment terms; the price or method if the resource is ever bought into the commons; how co-stewards split their say over free spending (equal unless stated).

**Its stewards.** Names; the decision method they use; who is the contact for the Board.

## Activity Sheet

*Attached to [[06 Agreement - Running an Activity]]. Half a page.*

**The activity.** Name; which kind (course or retreat / project for a client / venture); what it does and for whom; which need in the network it answers, with its entry in the register of needs (Rulebook 6.4).

**The people.** The activity stewards; the decision method; who is the contact for the Board.

**The resources.** Which lent place and assets it uses, if any, with their stewards' written consent, and how the resource slice splits among them (all to the place unless stated; Rulebook 4.2).

**Who invoices.** The Association (default), or a named partner's company, which then pays the slices to the Association as a service fee (Rulebook 4.5).

**The money.** For a course: the three price bands, the number of cost-only places, and the teacher's share (fixed fee / revenue share / hybrid); the minimum number of paying places below which it is rescheduled. For a project or venture: how contributions are recorded and validated, any fixed share reserved for coordination and care, the phase (first phase: network as the only market; then the share of capacity that may serve outside), and any agreed running costs.

**The dates.** When it runs, and when it ends or is reviewed.

## Welcome Letter

*Attached to [[04 Agreement - Joining]]. Not a form. Written by you as a letter to the people you are joining, or, if you prefer, written down by a steward while you talk over a meal on your first day. Both are equal.*

Tell us who you are and what brought you here. Tell us what you would love to bring: skills, experience, tools, the things you know how to grow or make or teach. Tell us what you need to be well: your rhythms, your food, any health matter we should know about, what kind of rest you need. Tell us what you would like to learn, and what you would like to build in the world, so that we know where to point the group's attention. Tell us anything that would help us welcome you.

*For the steward, at the foot of the letter, in a few plain lines so they can be found at seven in the morning:* allergies and health notes · emergency contact · dates of stay · insurance acknowledged · safeguarding policy read.
