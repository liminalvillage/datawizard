# Rulebook

*Every rule of the network, stated once. The four agreements point here instead of repeating it. The [[Statute - ReGenerativa APS|Statute]] governs over this Rulebook; this Rulebook governs over the sheets and any procedure. It is the Association's internal regulation under Article 12 of the Statute and is adopted and amended by the Board; changes apply forward, never to a settlement already closed. Figures live only in section 11.*

---

## 1. Words we use

- **The Association** - ReGenerativa APS, the one legal person. Everything below happens inside it.
- **Resource** - anything lent to the Association for its purpose. A resource is either a place or an asset. Every resource has an owner, at least one steward, and its own envelope.
- **Place** - a resource you can be in: a house, land, a workshop. A place that people can live or gather in is a **hub**.
- **Asset** - a resource that is a thing: a vehicle, a tool, equipment, a library, a herd, and the like.
- **Owner** - whoever lends a resource. They keep owning it.
- **Member** - a person who has joined the Association. One member, one vote.
- **Steward** - a member who cares for a resource, or runs an activity, day to day. A resource can have several stewards together.
- **Activity** - anything that brings money in: a course, a retreat, a project for a client, a venture.
- **Holon** - the accounting and decision unit for one resource or one activity: its own envelope inside the Association's single account, directed by its stewards. The Association itself is a holon too.
- **Net earnings** - what an activity takes in, less VAT, payment charges, and the direct cost of delivering it (food, materials, travel, venue paid to third parties). The slice is taken from this.
- **The slice** - the share of any net earnings that goes to the network, and to the borrowed resources the earnings used.
- **The pool** - the one shared reserve the Association holds, fed by surplus, drawn on by need.
- **Your record** - the running, portable list of what you have contributed, across every resource and role, for as long as you are part of the network. In 1.0 this was the Network Passport.

## 2. Resources

2.1 **Lending.** A resource enters the network when its owner lends it to the Association for free (comodato, Art. 1803 Civil Code) under [[03 Agreement - Lending a Resource]], with a **Resource Sheet** ([[07 Sheets]]) saying what it is, what it may be used for, any conditions, and the basics of caring for it. The owner keeps ownership and can take the resource back with notice (section 8). Ordinary upkeep is the network's; structural repairs and major overhauls are the owner's, unless the Resource Sheet says otherwise.

2.2 **Care.** Every resource has at least one steward under [[05 Agreement - Caring for a Resource]]. The steward keeps the resource sound and used for its purpose, holds its keys and records, and directs its money within section 4. Where the resource is a place, the steward also welcomes the people who come and keeps its culture (2.3); where it is an asset, the steward keeps it maintained, stored, insured and available to the network on published terms. Co-stewards hold one mandate together and decide by the method they declare on the Resource Sheet.

2.3 **Members in residence outnumber guests.** A hub keeps its culture through the people who stay. On any night, the members in residence at a hub (its stewards plus any member who has committed to stay for at least one of the hub's cycles, a month unless the Resource Sheet says a season) must outnumber the non-members sleeping there. Day visitors do not count. When one more guest would break the majority, the bed is either kept for a member, or the guest is invited to join. No one is pressured to join.

2.4 **From lent to owned.** A resource may in time pass into the Association's ownership: bought at a price agreed on the Resource Sheet, funded from the resource's own reserve line (section 4), or given. Once the Association owns a resource it can never be sold back into private hands; by law it can only pass to another mission entity.

2.5 **Groups of resources.** Resources that share a valley, a watershed, a purpose or a place may group into a larger holon for sharing tools, people and surplus. A group runs the same money shape as any holon and holds no vote of its own.

## 3. People

3.1 **Joining.** Anyone who shares the purpose may join under [[04 Agreement - Joining]] by written application and the annual fee. The Board decides without discrimination and gives reasons for any refusal. Membership is for as long as you want it; you may leave at any time.

3.2 **One member, one vote.** Every member of three months' standing votes in the Assembly and may stand for office. No contribution, gift, role, or length of service changes that.

3.3 **Every member may tend.** Membership carries a standing invitation to look after a project, a corner of a place, or an asset, as much or as little as you choose. Where you actively tend a resource, your recent contribution there gives you a proportionate say in how that resource's free money is spent (section 4.4). That is administration, not a vote.

3.4 **Roles stack.** A member may also be an owner, a steward of one or more resources, and a steward of one or more activities, all at once. Each role is one short agreement. Taking up a new role never reopens the ones you hold.

3.5 **No role grants property, profit, or power.** No role gives anyone a right to anything the Association holds, a share of any surplus, or a vote beyond the one every member has. Everything held by any holon is the Association's.

3.6 **Independent work is free.** Everyone remains free to pursue their own professional, creative and business life outside the network. Network resources are used for the network's purpose only.

3.7 **Elders.** A member who has held a role for five years (three for those in the network by end of 2026) and whose record shows long, substantial care may be recognised as an elder at the annual gathering. Elders are hosted freely across the network, are the first people asked to mediate (section 9), and speak for the land and for future generations before any large allocation from the pool. Eldership is a recognition, not a contract, and carries no vote beyond the member's own.

3.8 **Other organisations.** A non-profit organisation may join as an entity member with one vote, cast by a named person. It keeps its own books and is not hosted by the Association; anything it does jointly with the network is set up as an ordinary activity under section 4. Entity members never exceed half the roll. A for-profit company may only be a client, a partner in an activity, or a giver.

3.9 **Conflicts of interest.** Whoever takes part in a decision declares any personal or economic interest in it first and abstains where the interest is direct. Where the same person holds two roles that face each other (for example steward of a place and runner of an activity on it), the Board names another member to speak for the second role.

## 4. Money - the shape

4.1 **One account, many envelopes.** All money of every resource and activity passes through the Association's single account on its fiscal-hosting platform, where every inflow and expense is public to members. Each holon has its own envelope there. The Association is the legal person for everything; no holon can bind it beyond its own envelope.

4.2 **The shape.** Whenever a holon earns money, it moves through the same steps, in this order:

1. **The slice.** Off the top of net earnings, one share goes to the Association's envelope (the **network slice**). If the earnings used lent resources, a second share (the **resource slice**) goes to their envelopes, split as the Activity Sheet says; if it says nothing, all of it goes to the place used, or equally among the assets used if no place was. Both shares are fixed in section 11, uniform for everyone, not negotiable per activity. The slice applies once per inflow; transfers between envelopes carry no further slice.
2. **Costs first.** The holon covers its own running costs: upkeep, insurance, materials, and the steward's approved expenses (section 4.6).
3. **A reserve for renewal.** For a resource: what is left accrues to a protected line toward replacing the resource if it is lost, or buying it (section 2.4), up to the target on the Resource Sheet. Stewards cannot spend this line on anything else.
4. **Stewards direct the rest.** Up to the ceiling in section 11, the holon's stewards spend what remains on clearly stated proposals within the holon's purpose. No one upstream pre-approves in-scope spending.
5. **Surplus rises.** Anything above the ceiling flows to the pool.

*When you receive money from a workshop you hosted at a lent farmhouse: it lands in your activity's envelope; the network slice and the resource slice move automatically; you cover your costs; what is left is yours to direct (or to share among the people who did the work, section 4.5) up to the ceiling; above that it goes to the pool. Nothing else to do.*

4.3 **The Association is an ordinary holon.** The Association's own envelope, stewarded by the Board, runs the same steps: the network slice covers the network's shared costs (administration, insurance, the platform), the Board directs what is left to network programmes and to topping up resources that fall short, and anything above its own ceiling goes to the pool. The centre cannot hoard.

4.4 **Settling over time.** Envelopes settle monthly. A holon that cannot cover its costs in a month carries the gap forward and its free spending narrows in proportion, healing over the following months rather than being clawed back. Where co-stewards share a holon, they split the say over free spending equally unless the Resource or Activity Sheet declares another split based on recorded contribution.

4.5 **Paying people is separate.** Nothing in the shape is income to anyone. Where a client project is invoiced by a partner's own company rather than by the Association (recorded on the Activity Sheet), that company pays the network slice and the resource slice to the Association as an invoiced service fee, and the shape applies to what the Association receives. Where someone is paid for work (teaching, building, facilitating, delivering a project), that is a separate written agreement, priced as services, invoiced or documented under Italian fiscal rules, with the Association acting as withholding agent where required. Stewardship of a resource is voluntary; it may be paired with paid services, but is never itself paid. In an activity, the people who did the work share what is left after the slice and costs in proportion to their recorded, validated contribution (section 5.2), each documenting their own income.

4.6 **Steward expenses.** Stewards are reimbursed only for real, documented, role-related expenses, item by item against receipts, within 60 days, from the holon's costs line. Self-certified small expenses only within the limits of Art. 17(4) of the Third Sector Code.

4.7 **The pool.** The pool is the Association's one reserve, kept as its own envelope, never merged with the network's running costs. It **catches**: a holon in genuine, sustained shortfall beyond the months of tolerance in section 11 is supported from it, so that no resource fails for lack of cover alone; first the holon's costs target is re-tuned, and only if shortfall persists are its stewards changed, under the protections of section 8. It **rewards**: a holon that feeds the pool earns first call on it and a longer leash; its stewards' records note the contribution. Every allocation from the pool is decided by the Board with a two-thirds majority, posted to members within 14 days, and open to question by any three members, who receive a public answer within 30 days. Before any large allocation, one member is asked to speak for the land and for future generations.

4.8 **Gifts.** Anyone may give money, tools, materials, land or time to the network as a gift. Ownership passes to the Association; the giver acquires no say over how it is used beyond a stated purpose, which the Association honours or declines. Gifts earmarked to a resource or activity land in its envelope; other gifts land in the Association's. Gifts are recorded and thanked, in public if the giver wishes, but **money given never counts toward access** (section 5.4). A donor's restriction always prevails over the shape. Grants and public calls are applied for by the Board and signed by the President; a holon may propose one.

4.9 **Hard rails.** Every allocator, at every scale: decides within purpose and under the ceiling; spends only on what serves both the holon's purpose and the Association's mission; needs a second signature above the amount in section 11 and always on their own reimbursement; never allocates to their own benefit; reconciles the envelope at handover so a successor inherits a known balance; and never, at any scale, distributes profit or patrimony to anyone (Art. 8, Third Sector Code). The Association reports every flow annually in its social report, and the Board watches, each quarter, that earning activity stays within the limits that keep the Association non-commercial under Art. 79 of the Third Sector Code, keeping restricted gifts, grants and earnings in separate lines.

4.10 **Ventures that leave.** An activity that grows into its own legal entity may leave with the Association's written agreement, on a two-thirds vote of its members. It settles what it owes and keeps paying the network slice on what it earns, at a rate fixed when it leaves within the band in section 11, as a service fee invoiced by the Association. Nothing is distributed to departing members.

## 5. Contribution and access

5.1 **Your record.** Everything you give, in any role at any resource, is recorded in your record and confirmed by a steward or peer in good faith. It is yours, it is portable, it never resets, and it has no tiers or ranks. You may see, correct, or delete it at any time; when a role ends you receive a written summary within 30 days.

5.2 **Three readings.** Your **lifetime** record decides your access (5.4). Your **recent** contribution to one resource decides your say over that resource's free spending (3.3). Where an activity's earnings are shared among the people who did the work, their **validated contributions to that activity** decide the shares (4.5). Euro amounts used for that third reading never feed the first.

5.3 **Members carry the resources.** Using a resource or a programme is never free by default: whoever stays, eats, works or learns there, or takes a tool or vehicle out, contributes to its running costs, in money, in work, or in both, on the terms the resource's steward publishes. The access rule (5.4) decides how far that contribution slides toward free; the floor (5.5) guarantees that inability to pay in money is never, alone, a reason to turn a member away where the place has room.

5.4 **The access rule.** The more you have contributed, the higher your priority and the lower your price for what the network offers, up to and including free. Each steward decides, for the resource they hold, how many places are free and how price slides, weighing contribution together with need, mission fit, and rotation, so that access serves the purpose rather than compounding advantage. There is no conversion rate and no perk table. Doubt is resolved toward wider access. Money given never counts.

5.5 **The floor.** Every member may book stays and programme places ahead of the public, and is never turned away from a place with spare capacity only for inability to pay. The floor costs the pool nothing; it is foregone revenue at the place.

5.6 **Attunement.** Where a score or an allocation produces an outcome that someone senses betrays the gift, anyone affected may ask for a facilitated circle before it is final. The circle pauses the outcome once, for up to 14 days, and may recommend, never decide.

## 6. Decisions

6.1 **Each holon chooses its method.** Consensus, sociocracy, majority, or another: declared on its sheet, with every member of the holon able to speak and take part, and decisions recorded. A single-person holon decides alone under its steward's responsibility.

6.2 **Only the Board or Assembly may:** amend the Statute or this Rulebook; approve the accounts; sign contracts that bind the Association to third parties (leases, comodato, employment, loans); incur debt beyond an envelope; touch the minimum patrimony; apply to public calls; admit a new resource or a new member; transfer any right, mark, data, or anything the Association holds. Anything done by a holon in breach of this is not enforceable against the Association and may make its author personally liable.

6.3 **Stewards meet.** All stewards of active resources and activities meet at least quarterly to share status, needs, and overloads, and to propose cooperation. The circle has no power of its own; its proposals go to the Board. It keeps a short record visible to members.

6.4 **Admitting an activity.** A venture starts from a need the network has recorded: any member may post a need to the open register of needs on the platform; a venture names the need it answers on its Activity Sheet, and the Board confirms the need is real and unmet before the venture's envelope is opened.

6.4A **Admitting a resource.** A new resource enters on the Board's decision after a check that it fits the mission and that the network has the capacity to care for it. The first six months are provisional.

6.5 **Changing the rules.** The Board proposes any change to this Rulebook or to the numbers in section 11, publishes it to all members at least 30 days before adopting it, and hears objections; the Assembly ratifies the numbers table each year with the accounts. Changes apply forward only. A signed agreement keeps the figures it was signed with unless the signer accepts the new ones.

6.6 **Transparency.** Every envelope is public to members on the platform. Each holon sends the Board a short annual note: use, activity, cost status, allocations, and how the resource is doing against the two promises of the [[01 Charter]].

## 7. Support arrangements

Where the network carries a person while they build a livelihood, the two write down, before it starts: what support is given and for how long; the hours per week the person will spend on their own livelihood in the wider world, agreed together and revisable whenever capacity changes; and, if the venture prospers, what bounded part of its value returns to the commons (scope, income basis, portion, threshold, duration, cap). Five things never vary: belonging is not productivity; support is not debt; capacity moves and belonging is not withdrawn when it narrows; the return is bounded and creates no equity, loan, or claim; and where money moves, section 4 governs. Support is given through the practice of one person's challenge at a time before the group, through mentoring, introductions, tools and, where money moves, through section 4. *The legal form and tax treatment of the return are pending advice; no arrangement with a return is signed until the Board ratifies the instrument (section 11).*

## 8. Leaving

8.1 **An owner takes a resource back** with the notice in section 11. Stays and activities already confirmed are honoured within the notice. A resident steward is told within 14 days and is helped to find a place within the network. If the owner sells, the buyer is offered the chance to continue; if not, the network leaves in the agreed condition. The resource's reserve line moves with its stewards to a replacement resource rather than to the pool.

8.2 **A steward steps down** with the notice in section 11, hands over keys, records and a reconciled envelope, and completes or hands on anything scheduled within the notice.

8.3 **A member leaves** at any time by writing to the Board; the fee for the current year stays due. Their record stays theirs.

8.4 **An activity ends** by its members' decision, by twelve months' inactivity, or by Board decision for serious cause. The envelope is reconciled; restricted funds follow the funder; the rest goes to the pool.

8.5 **For serious cause**, either side may end any agreement at once, in writing with reasons, after the other side has been heard unless safety requires otherwise. Serious cause is: danger to people; misappropriation of money, keys or records; use of a resource against its purpose or the owner's conditions; persistent disregard of the Association's lawful instructions; conduct incompatible with the [[01 Charter]]. Ending for cause is reviewable under section 9.

## 9. When things go wrong

9.1 **Safety first.** Any concern for a person's safety goes straight to a Designated Safeguarding Lead under the [[Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct]], which takes precedence over this Rulebook. The Board may suspend any role or any holon's autonomy as a precaution, for a stated period, in writing.

9.2 **Disputes.** In order: (1) a direct conversation; (2) after a pause of at least 48 hours, unless safety or a legal deadline says otherwise, a facilitated conversation with a trusted member who is not a steward of that resource; (3) three uninvolved members or stewards, elders first, convened by the Board within 30 days, giving a non-binding opinion; where the Assembly has elected a Collegio dei Probiviri under Statute Art. 16, that body; (4) external mediation under Legislative Decree 28/2010; (5) the Court of Ascoli Piceno. Participants and guests who are not members may use the same ladder.

9.3 **Complaints from the public** go to the steward of the resource, then to the Board, and are answered in writing within 30 days.

## 10. If the Association ends

If the Association is dissolved, what it owns passes to another Third Sector entity as the law requires. The liquidators seek a successor that will carry the network; the records of every member's contribution are handed to it, or, failing a successor within 180 days, archived publicly. People come first within what the law leaves: resident stewards keep their notice window, activities in flight are finished or handed on, and the pool remains mutual support until it is empty. Lending agreements end automatically, and each owner is asked to witness the community that grew around what they lent rather than to serve it.
